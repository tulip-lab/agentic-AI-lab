# Public sample case: Monthly average-salary reports

A fictional company has 137 employees. Each month it publishes the employee count and the average salary. The reports contain no names or individual salaries.

A single overall average does not normally reveal one person's salary. Across several months, however, a reader might combine the reports with outside knowledge, such as one known hire or departure, and try to infer an individual's contribution. Whether an exact salary can be inferred depends on the reported totals, changes in membership, and other known facts. Do not claim that repeated averages always disclose an exact salary.

The learning activity compares a one-time report with a sequence of reports. Learners propose limits on publication frequency, reporting groups, or data detail, and discuss the limits of each. Differential privacy is a formal method for further study, but this case specifies no mechanism or privacy-budget value; do not claim the reports are differentially private.
